# 21 — Canonical token bridge

## 1. Overview

This document specifies the canonical token bridge of codex ADR 0013 between an OrangeSDK chain and Ethereum, built on the two verified message channels without changing their trust model: messages from the OrangeSDK chain to Ethereum go through the outbox, a light client of threshold certificates and `OrangeInbox` (spec 17); messages from Ethereum to the OrangeSDK chain go through `EthereumOutbox`, the sync-committee light client and the inbox (spec 18). The decisions behind this document are recorded in codex ADR 0034; the rate limits of §6.5 and the handshake of §5.7 in codex ADR 0041.

One *token gateway* runs on each chain, and the two are paired: each accepts messages only from the other. A token native to a chain is *locked* in that chain's gateway and *minted* on the other chain as a *wrapped token* that the other gateway creates; burning the wrapped token *releases* the locked token. This works in both directions: ETH and Ethereum's ERC-20s become wrapped tokens on the OrangeSDK chain, and the chain's own currency and its ERC-20s become wrapped tokens on Ethereum.

Integers in messages and ABI words are big-endian.

## 2. Tokens and where they live

A gateway distinguishes two kinds of token:

- A **native token** of its chain: any ERC-20 there, or the chain's currency, which the gateway names `NATIVE`, the zero address. A deposit of a native token *locks* it in the gateway.
- A **wrapped token**: a `BridgedToken` this gateway created for a token native to the other chain, its *origin*. A deposit of a wrapped token *burns* it.

ETH arrives on the OrangeSDK chain as the wrapped token of origin `NATIVE`, an ERC-20 named `Ether` with symbol `ETH` and 18 decimals, not as the OrangeSDK chain's currency. The OrangeSDK chain's currency is the chain's own: it pays gas and bonds stake (spec 15), and its supply is the chain's to govern. Minting it against ETH locked on Ethereum would put the currency that secures the chain at the mercy of the sync-committee trust of spec 18 §2, and a forged message could mint the stake that decides who validates. As an ordinary ERC-20, wrapped ETH is bounded by its own cap (§6.2) and harms only its holders if the channel is ever forged.

The OrangeSDK chain's currency arrives on Ethereum as the wrapped token of origin `NATIVE` there, named and symbolled as the OrangeSDK-chain gateway was deployed (§5.2), with 18 decimals. The OrangeSDK-chain gateway holds the currency it locks: it does not send it as the value of an outbox message, because the outbox keeps such value for ever (spec 17 §2.3) and the return trip could not release it. Every gateway message carries value 0.

## 3. Messages

A gateway's message is an ordinary message of the channel it sends through, from the gateway to the paired gateway; its payload is one of

```
MINT    = abi.encode(uint8 1, address origin, address from, address to, uint256 amount,
                     string name, string symbol, uint8 decimals)
RELEASE = abi.encode(uint8 2, address token, address from, address to, uint256 amount)
HELLO   = abi.encode(uint8 3, bool heard)
REFUND  = abi.encode(uint8 4, uint64 nonce, uint8 kind, address token, address from, address to,
                     uint256 amount)
SPENT   = abi.encode(uint8 5, uint64 nonce, bytes32 effect)
```

`MINT` asks the receiving gateway to mint `amount` of the wrapped token of `origin` (a token native to the sending chain, which `from` locked there) to `to`; `name`, `symbol` and `decimals` describe `origin` and name the wrapped token if this mint creates it (§4). `RELEASE` asks it to release `amount` of `token`, native to the receiving chain and locked there, to `to`; `from` burned the wrapped token on the sending chain. `HELLO` says the sending gateway exists, and `heard` whether a message of the receiving gateway has reached it (§5.7). `REFUND` asks the receiving gateway to give back to `from` the value of message `nonce`, a `MINT` or `RELEASE` (`kind`) that it sent naming `token`, `from`, `to` and `amount`, and that the sending gateway took out of its channel with no effect: queued and cancelled by its owner (§5.5b), or a `MINT` of a token it does not list (§5.4). `SPENT` tells the receiving gateway that the sending gateway's inbox spent nonce `nonce` of the receiving gateway's messages, by a message of identity `effect` (§5.3) or by nothing that took effect (`VOID`, the word 1), so that whatever else the receiving gateway sent with that nonce never arrives (§5.5c). The channel's nonce identifies a message; the payload carries no nonce of its own, except the nonce a `REFUND` or `SPENT` names, which is that of the message it refunds or reports.

The gateways rely on exactly what the channels guarantee: a message is delivered at most once (spec 17 §6.1, spec 18 §6.5), its sender is authenticated, and a delivery whose recipient reverts is undone and may be retried. They do not rely on order: messages may be delivered in any order, which is why every `MINT` carries the metadata (§4) rather than only a token's first deposit.

`TokenMessage` in `orange_bridge::tokens` encodes and decodes the five payloads; decoding accepts exactly the encoding above, which is what a gateway sends. `identity(payload)` computes the identity a sending gateway records for a payload (§5.3).

## 4. Wrapped tokens

`BridgedToken` is an ERC-20 (`name`, `symbol`, `decimals`, `totalSupply`, `balanceOf`, `allowance`, `transfer`, `approve`, `transferFrom`, the `Transfer` and `Approval` events) whose constructor takes no arguments and records its creator as `gateway`. Only the gateway calls `mint(to, amount)` and `burn(from, amount)`, which burns without an allowance, and `initialize(origin, name, symbol, decimals)`, once; anyone else gets `NotGateway()`, a second initialization `AlreadyInitialized()`. An allowance of `2^256 − 1` is never decreased. Transfers to the zero address revert `ZeroAddress()`; transfers and burns beyond a balance revert `InsufficientBalance()`, `transferFrom` beyond an allowance `InsufficientAllowance()`.

A gateway creates the wrapped token of `origin` with `CREATE2`, with salt `origin` as a 32-byte word, in the delivery of the first `MINT` of `origin` it receives, and initializes it with that message's metadata in the same call. Its address is therefore

```
wrapped_token(gateway, origin) = keccak256(0xff ‖ gateway ‖ uint256(origin) ‖ keccak256(BridgedToken creation code))[12..32]
```

which anyone computes before the token exists: `wrappedToken(origin)` on the gateway, or `orange_bridge::tokens::wrapped_token`. The creation code is the committed fixture `contracts/build/BridgedToken.bin`; the gateway stores its hash as `tokenCodeHash`. There is one wrapped token per origin and gateway, and no other way to create one.

The metadata a deposit carries (§5.3) is the origin's `name()` and `symbol()`, each an ABI string or a `bytes32` (up to its first zero byte), cut to its first `MAX_METADATA = 64` bytes, and empty if the call fails or returns anything else; and `decimals()` if it returns one word below 256, else 18. For `NATIVE` it is the gateway's native name and symbol and 18. Later changes of an origin's metadata do not reach its wrapped token.

## 5. Gateways

Both gateways are `TokenGateway`; the two subcontracts only send and authenticate messages.

### 5.1 `TokenGatewayOnEthereum`

Constructor `(outbox, inbox, orangeChainId, orangeGateway, owner, guardian, messageGas)`: the OrangeSDK chain's `EthereumOutbox`, the `OrangeInbox` delivering that chain's messages, the chain's id, the paired gateway on the OrangeSDK chain, the roles of §5.6 and the gas each message gives the gateway on the OrangeSDK chain (spec 18 §6.5). It reverts `ZeroAddress()` if the outbox, the inbox, the paired gateway, the owner or the guardian is zero, and `MessageGasTooLow()` if `messageGas` is below `MIN_MESSAGE_GAS = 1 000 000`. Its native name and symbol are `Ether` and `ETH`.

It sends with `outbox.send(orangeGateway, messageGas, payload)`, the first time from its constructor, a `HELLO` (§5.7). `messageGas` must cover the first `MINT` of a token, which creates the wrapped token: that delivery uses about 720 000 gas more than a mint of an existing token (§8); the tests use 1 500 000. The floor `MIN_MESSAGE_GAS` keeps the owner from setting a gas that strands messages: a `RELEASE` needs far less, and a `MINT` sent with too little gas for creation would stay undeliverable only until another message of the same origin, with enough gas, had created the token, after which its mint is cheap. The OrangeSDK chain's block gas limit must admit a delivery with `messageGas` (spec 18 §6.5).

`onOrangeMessage(sourceChainId, nonce, sender, value, payload)` (`IOrangeMessageRecipient`) reverts `NotInbox()` unless the caller is `inbox`, `WrongChain()` unless `sourceChainId` is `orangeChainId`, `NotRemoteGateway()` unless `sender` is the paired gateway and `WrongValue()` unless `value` is 0; it then receives the payload (§5.4).

The owner may call `setInbox(inbox)` (never zero), which moves the gateway to a successor `OrangeInbox` (spec 17 §6.4), and `setMessageGas(gas)` (at least `MIN_MESSAGE_GAS`), for messages sent from then on.

Anyone may tell the gateway on the OrangeSDK chain what `inbox` did with a nonce of its messages (§5.5c), once per nonce: `reported[nonce]` is set, a second report reverts `AlreadyReported()`, and the gateway sends `SPENT(nonce, effect)` as its own records do (§5.3), logging `Reported(nonce, effect, reportNonce)` and returning `reportNonce` (the gateway on the OrangeSDK chain reports the other way in the same way, §5.2):

- `reportVetoed(nonce)`: `inbox.entries(nonce)` has status `Vetoed` (spec 17 §6.3), else `NotVetoed()`; `effect` is `VOID`, since nothing will ever be delivered with that nonce.
- `reportDelivered(Message m)`: `inbox.entries(m.nonce)` has status `Delivered` and the hash `inbox.hashMessage(m)`, else `NotDelivered()`; so `m` is exactly the message the inbox delivered with that nonce. `effect` is `identity(m.payload)` (§5.3) if `m.recipient` is this gateway, and `VOID` otherwise, as a message to anyone else took no effect here.

Both read the inbox's own record, which the inbox keeps for every nonce it proved, so a report states what the inbox did, whatever the light client that proved the message. Neither moves value or reads the pause.

### 5.2 `TokenGatewayOnOrange`

Constructor `(outbox, inbox, ethereumChainId, ethereumGateway, owner, guardian, nativeName, nativeSymbol)`. On an OrangeSDK chain the outbox is `0x0000000000000000000000000000000000004f42` and the inbox `0x0000000000000000000000000000000000004549`; `OrangeGatewayConfig::new` fills them in. They are constructor arguments, not constants, so that the same code runs against stand-ins in tests. It reverts `ZeroAddress()` if the outbox, the inbox, the paired gateway, the owner or the guardian is zero. `nativeName` and `nativeSymbol` name the chain's currency on Ethereum.

It sends with `outbox.send(ethereumChainId, ethereumGateway, payload)` and no value, which costs `send_gas(n)` (spec 17 §2.4), the first time from its constructor, a `HELLO` (§5.7). `onEthereumMessage(nonce, sender, payload)` (`IEthereumMessageReceiver`) reverts `NotInbox()` unless the caller is the inbox and `NotRemoteGateway()` unless `sender` is the paired gateway, then receives the payload (§5.4). The inbox and outbox are fixed: they are the chain's system addresses, which only a kernel upgrade changes.

Anyone may tell the gateway on Ethereum what the inbox delivered with a nonce of its messages (§5.5c), once per nonce, as `reportDelivered` on Ethereum does (§5.1), with the same `reported`, `AlreadyReported()` and `Reported` log:

- `reportDelivered(EthereumInbox.Message m)`, `m` being `(uint64 nonce, address sender, address recipient, uint64 gasLimit, bytes payload)`: `inbox.deliveredHash(m.nonce)` equals `inbox.hashMessage(m)` (spec 18 §6.5), else `NotDelivered()`; so `m` is exactly the message the inbox delivered with that nonce, its gas limit included. `effect` is `identity(m.payload)` if `m.recipient` is this gateway, and `VOID` otherwise.

It reads the record the verifier keeps of every nonce it delivered, the message's hash (spec 18 §6.4). The inbox has no veto, so there is no `reportVetoed` here: a nonce is spent on the OrangeSDK chain only by a delivery.

### 5.3 Deposits

`deposit(address token, address to, uint256 amount) payable returns (uint64 nonce)` bridges `amount` of `token` to `to` on the other chain and returns the channel's nonce of the message. It reverts `IsPaused()` while paused, `Reentrant()` inside another call into the gateway, `PeerUnconfirmed()` until a message of the paired gateway has arrived (§5.7), `ZeroAddress()` if `to` is zero and `ZeroAmount()` if `amount` is 0. Then:

- **A wrapped token of this gateway**: `WrongValue()` if the call carries value; the token burns `amount` from the caller (reverting `InsufficientBalance()` beyond its balance), and the message is `RELEASE(originOf[token], caller, to, amount)`.
- **Any other token**, native: `CapExceeded(token)` if `locked[token] + amount` exceeds `lockCap[token]`. For `NATIVE` the call's value must be `amount`; for an ERC-20 it must be 0 (`WrongValue()` otherwise), the gateway calls `transferFrom(caller, gateway, amount)` and reverts `TransferFailed()` if that reverts or returns anything but nothing or `true`, and `BalanceMismatch()` unless its own `balanceOf` grew by exactly `amount`. `locked[token]` grows by `amount`, and the message is `MINT(token, caller, to, amount, metadata)` (§4).

The gateway sends the message (§5.1, §5.2), records what it asked as `sent[nonce] = keccak256(abi.encode(kind, token', caller, to, amount))`, with `kind` and `token'` the message's kind and the token it names (`originOf[token]` for a `RELEASE`, `token` for a `MINT`), so that it can recognise the message in a refund (§5.5b), and logs `Deposited(nonce, token, caller, to, amount)`. For a `RELEASE` the record is the hash of the payload itself.

That record is the message's *identity*: `identity(payload)` is `keccak256` of the first five words of a `MINT` payload, leaving out the metadata, and of the whole payload of any other message. A gateway records the identity of every message that carries value or a claim: each `MINT` and `RELEASE` a deposit sends, each `REFUND` it sends (§5.4, §5.5b) and each `SPENT` (§5.1), under the message's nonce; never a `HELLO`, which its owner sends again instead (§5.7). The identity of a `MINT` or `RELEASE` hashes five words and that of a `REFUND` or `SPENT` seven or three, so no record of one kind can be taken for one of another.

### 5.4 Receiving

A message the subcontract authenticated is acted on as follows; the whole delivery reverts, and the message stays undelivered for anyone to deliver again (§3), if the gateway is paused (`IsPaused()`), on reentrance (`Reentrant()`), for a payload whose first word is no kind (`UnknownMessage()`), for one shorter than a word or that does not decode as its kind (empty revert data), or as each case says. Whatever its kind, the first message accepted proves the paired gateway exists: `peerLive` becomes true and the gateway logs `PeerConfirmed()` (§5.7).

- **`MINT(origin, from, to, amount, name, symbol, decimals)`** of an origin whose `mintCap[origin]` is zero, a token this gateway does not list (§6.2): the gateway sends it back, `REFUND(nonce, MINT, origin, from, to, amount)` recorded as §5.3 says, and logs `Bounced(nonce, refundNonce)`; nothing else happens here, no wrapped token is created, and the message counts as delivered. The sending gateway refunds it as it refunds a cancelled transfer (§5.5b).
- **`MINT(origin, from, to, amount, name, symbol, decimals)`** of a listed origin: the wrapped token of `origin`, created and initialized with the metadata if it does not exist (logging `WrappedTokenCreated(origin, token, name, symbol, decimals)`); `CapExceeded(origin)` if its `totalSupply` plus `mintsQueued[origin]` plus `amount` exceeds `mintCap[origin]`. Then the mint limit of `origin` schedules `amount` (§6.5): if it is due now, the gateway mints `amount` to `to` and logs `Minted(nonce, origin, token, from, to, amount)`; otherwise `mintsQueued[origin]` grows by `amount` and the mint is queued (§5.5a).
- **`RELEASE(token, from, to, amount)`**: `locked[token]` shrinks by `amount` (a panic if it would go below zero, which a message of the paired gateway never asks). Then the release limit of `token` schedules `amount` (§6.5): if it is not due now, `releasesQueued[token]` grows by `amount` and the release is queued (§5.5a); if it is, the gateway *releases* it: `InsufficientGas()` if less than `⌊TRANSFER_GAS · 64 / 63⌋ + TRANSFER_RESERVE` gas is left, with `TRANSFER_GAS = 100 000` and `TRANSFER_RESERVE = 60 000`; then it sends `amount` to `to` with `TRANSFER_GAS` gas: the currency by a plain call with value and no data, an ERC-20 by `transfer(to, amount)`, copying at most one word of return data. The release succeeds if the call does not revert and returns a word equal to 1, or returns nothing (for the currency, or from a token that has code), and logs `Released(nonce, token, to, from, amount)`. Otherwise `owed[token][to]` grows by `amount` and it logs `ReleaseFailed(nonce, token, to, from, amount)`; the message counts as delivered.
- **`HELLO(heard)`**: if `heard` is false, the gateway answers with `HELLO(true)`; otherwise nothing more.
- **`REFUND(original, kind, token, from, to, amount)`**: paid if this gateway sent exactly that message and has not refunded it, refused otherwise (§5.5b).
- **`SPENT(original, effect)`**: recorded in `spent[original]` (§5.5c).

The gas check keeps a relayer, who chooses the gas of an `OrangeInbox` execution, from making a release fail on purpose and so pushing it into `owed`.

### 5.5 Owed releases

`claim(token, recipient)` sends `owed[token][recipient]` to `recipient`; anyone may call it. `claimTo(token, receiver)` sends what the caller is owed to `receiver` instead, for a recipient that can call but cannot receive. Each reverts `IsPaused()` while paused, `Reentrant()` on reentrance, `ZeroAddress()` for a zero receiver, `NothingOwed()` if nothing is owed and `InsufficientGas()` as in §5.4; it zeroes the debt, sends it as §5.4 does and reverts `TransferFailed()`, restoring the debt, if the transfer fails, else logs `Claimed(token, recipient, receiver, amount)`. A failed release is therefore never lost: the gateway keeps holding it, and it stays claimable for ever.

### 5.5a Queued transfers

A mint or release its limit does not allow now (§6.5) is recorded as `queued[id] = Transfer(token, kind, frozen, refund, nonce, readyAt, from, to, amount)`, `token` being the origin of a mint and the native token of a release, `frozen` false, `refund` true only for a transfer that a `REFUND` queued (§5.5b), `id` the next of `queuedCount` (from 0), and logged as `Queued(id, nonce, kind, token, to, amount, readyAt)`; the message counts as delivered. `executeQueued(id)`, which anyone may call once `block.timestamp ≥ readyAt`, deletes the entry, logs `QueueExecuted(id)` and acts on it: a mint shrinks `mintsQueued[origin]` and mints to `to`, logging `Minted` with the message's nonce; a release shrinks `releasesQueued[token]` and releases as §5.4 does, with its gas check, owing the amount if the transfer fails. It reverts `IsPaused()` while paused, `Reentrant()` on reentrance, `NothingQueued()` for an id that holds nothing (never queued, executed or cancelled), `Frozen()` for a frozen entry and `NotReady(readyAt)` before its time; a revert leaves the entry as it was. Nobody can redirect a queued transfer, and it is never lost: the value stays locked or reserved under the cap until it is executed, or held where it was locked if it is cancelled.

One queued transfer can be stopped without stopping the gateway:

- `freezeQueued(id)`, by the guardian or the owner, sets `frozen`, so that `executeQueued(id)` reverts `Frozen()`, and logs `QueueFrozen(id, by)`. Everything else goes on: deposits, deliveries, claims and every other queued transfer. This is the guardian's answer to one suspect transfer, where `pause()` would stop them all.
- `unfreezeQueued(id)`, by the owner, clears `frozen` and logs `QueueUnfrozen(id)`; the transfer executes at its time, or at once if that has passed.
- `cancelQueued(id)`, by the owner, frozen or not, deletes the entry, *undoes what its delivery did here* and logs `QueueCancelled(id, nonce, kind, token, from, to, amount)`: a mint shrinks `mintsQueued[origin]`, so nothing is minted and its room under the mint cap is free again; a release shrinks `releasesQueued[token]` and grows `locked[token]` by its amount, so the gateway holds it as locked again. Either way the limit it was scheduled under gets its amount back, its `used` brought up to now and reduced by the amount (never below zero), so that transfers after it are not held behind it; transfers already queued keep their `readyAt`. Nothing is sent to anyone: `refundQueued(id)` (§5.5b) cancels in the same way and then asks the gateway that sent the message to give its value back.

Each reverts `NothingQueued()` for an id that holds nothing, `NotGuardian()` (freeze) or `NotOwner()` (unfreeze, cancel) for anyone else, and works while paused. Cancelling undoes, because cancellation exists for messages the channel should never have delivered, and the receiving gateway cannot tell those from honest ones. For a forged message the undo restores the gateway exactly as it was before the delivery, and both equalities of §6.1 hold as if it had never arrived. A refund decided here would turn the forgery into real value: returning a forged `MINT` as a `RELEASE` would pay out tokens that are really locked on the other chain, and returning a forged `RELEASE` as a `MINT` would mint wrapped tokens that nothing backs. For an honest transfer cancelled without a refund, the value stays held where it was locked, exactly like a message vetoed in its channel (§6.4): `minted ≤ locked` holds, the surplus is accounted as `locked` with nothing minted against it, and `QueueCancelled` records who sent what to whom. The gateway that sent the message can tell an honest message from a forged one, which is what §5.5b builds on. An honest transfer frozen by mistake is unfrozen rather than cancelled.

### 5.5b Refunds

A gateway knows what it sent: every deposit records `sent[nonce]`, the hash of the kind, token, sender, recipient and amount of its message, under the message's nonce (§5.3). A forged message is one the paired gateway never sent, so its record there is missing or names something else. The refund of a cancelled transfer is therefore decided by the gateway that sent it, against its record, and never by the gateway that cancelled it.

`refundQueued(id)`, by the owner, reverts as `cancelQueued(id)` does and `NotRefundable()` for a transfer whose `refund` flag is set, and works while paused. It cancels the transfer exactly as `cancelQueued(id)` does (§5.5a), then sends the paired gateway `REFUND(nonce, kind, token, from, to, amount)` with the transfer's message nonce and fields, recorded as §5.3 says, logs `RefundRequested(id, nonce, refundNonce)` and returns `refundNonce`, the channel's nonce of the refund. The owner need not know whether the transfer is honest: the refund of a forgery pays nothing.

A gateway receiving `REFUND(original, kind, token, from, to, amount)` as message `nonce` compares `sent[original]` with `keccak256(abi.encode(kind, token, from, to, amount))`:

- **Equal**: it clears `sent[original]`, logs `Refunded(nonce, original)` and gives the value back to `from`, with `nonce` as the message's nonce in what it logs. A `MINT` it sent locked `token` here: it releases `amount` to `from` as a `RELEASE(token, from, from, amount)` would (§5.4: `locked` shrinks, the release limit schedules it, the transfer is owed if it fails). A `RELEASE` it sent burned the wrapped token of `token`: it mints `amount` of it to `from` as a `MINT` would (§5.4: the mint cap refuses it and the delivery stays in its channel, the mint limit schedules it). A refund that its limit queues has `refund` set.
- **Otherwise**: it logs `RefundRefused(nonce, original)` and pays nothing; the message counts as delivered. That is a refund of a message the gateway never sent, of one that asked something else, or of one already refunded. But the `REFUND` still says that the paired gateway's inbox spent nonce `original` and that nothing took effect with it there, the delivery having been undone (or, for a bounced `MINT`, never acted on): whatever this gateway sent with that nonce never arrives. So it marks the nonce spent with `VOID` (§5.5c), which lets a message of its own that a forgery displaced be refunded.

So a refund pays at most once for each message the gateway really sent, only to the message's sender, only what it carried, and only after the receiving gateway took the transfer out of its queue and undid its delivery, or never acted on it: the message's value is never both delivered and refunded, as a channel delivers each nonce once and the queue entry is gone. A cancelled forgery of either kind can never be turned into value. The refund adds no power to a forged channel back to the sender: one that could forge the `REFUND` could forge a `RELEASE` or `MINT` as well, to anyone, while the `REFUND` can pay only a recorded sender, and it meets the same caps and limits. A transfer that a refund queued is not refunded in turn: its message is a `REFUND`, whose record on the other side is the hash of seven words and never matches the five a refund names, so its owner unfreezes or cancels it instead.

The record costs every deposit one new storage slot and a hash of five words, about 22 500 gas (§8); a refund clears its slot. A `REFUND` sent costs the same for its own record.

### 5.5c Messages that never arrive

A channel delivers each nonce at most once, and some honest messages are never delivered: a forgery that takes the nonce of an honest message still in flight consumes it (a forged root or certificate proves other contents under that nonce, spec 17 §6.1, spec 18 §2; in `OrangeInbox` only until its guardian revokes the forged root, after which the honest message is proven again and arrives, spec 17 §6.3), and `OrangeInbox`'s guardian vetoes a message, after which nothing is delivered with its nonce (spec 17 §6.3). The value of such a message stays held where it was locked or burned, on the gateway that sent it, with its record. The sending gateway cannot see the other chain; the receiving side can say what became of a nonce, and does so with a `SPENT`:

| Source of `SPENT(n, effect)` | Sent by | `effect` | Because |
|---|---|---|---|
| a `REFUND` naming `n` that does not match the record (§5.5b) | the receiving gateway's owner, through `refundQueued`, or a delivery of an unlisted `MINT` (§5.4) | `VOID` (implied) | the inbox delivered `n`, and the delivery was undone or never acted on |
| `reportVetoed(n)` on Ethereum (§5.1) | anyone | `VOID` | `OrangeInbox` vetoed `n` and will never deliver it |
| `reportDelivered(m)` on Ethereum (§5.1) | anyone | `identity(m.payload)` if `m` came to the gateway, else `VOID` | `OrangeInbox` delivered `m` with nonce `n` |
| `reportDelivered(m)` on the OrangeSDK chain (§5.2) | anyone | `identity(m.payload)` if `m` came to the gateway, else `VOID` | the OrangeSDK chain's inbox delivered `m` with nonce `n` |

A gateway receiving `SPENT(n, effect)`, or the `REFUND` of the first row, records `spent[n] = effect` and logs `NonceSpent(n, effect)`; an effect other than `VOID` does not replace one already recorded, and `VOID`, which says that nothing took effect with `n`, replaces any. Then the gateway's own message `n`, if it sent one whose identity differs from `spent[n]`, never arrives, and anyone may have it made good:

- `refundSpent(n, kind, token, from, to, amount)` reverts `NotRecorded()` unless `sent[n]` is `keccak256(abi.encode(kind, token, from, to, amount))`, and `NotSpent()` if `spent[n]` is zero or that same identity; then it clears `sent[n]`, logs `SpentRefunded(n)` and gives the value back to `from` exactly as a matching `REFUND` does (§5.5b), under the same limits and caps, with `n` as the nonce in what it logs and `refund` set on a queued entry. It reverts `IsPaused()` while paused and `Reentrant()` on reentrance; a revert (a cap, the gas check) leaves the record to try again. The arguments are those of the message's `Deposited` log.
- `resendSpent(n, payload)`, for a `REFUND` or `SPENT` the gateway sent, reverts `NotRecorded()` unless `sent[n]` is `keccak256(payload)`, `NotResendable()` unless `payload` is a `REFUND` or `SPENT`, and `NotSpent()` as above; then it clears `sent[n]`, sends `payload` again, recorded under its new nonce, and logs `Resent(n, newNonce)`. Neither message moves value here, and both are safe to deliver more than once: a refund pays once against its record, and a nonce once spent stays spent.

**Why a refund here never pays for a message that is delivered, or can still be.** Assume the channel from the paired gateway back to this one is honest, so that every `SPENT` and `REFUND` this gateway receives was sent by the paired gateway; the channel this gateway's message `n` travels may be forged. The paired gateway says `n` is spent only from a fact about its own inbox, which has delivered nonce `n` once or will never deliver it:

1. *`VOID` from a `REFUND`.* The paired gateway's owner took the queue entry of a delivery with nonce `n` out of its queue and undid it (§5.5a), or the gateway sent back a `MINT` it does not list without acting on it. Either way the inbox has spent `n`, and nothing delivered with it took effect. This gateway's message `n`, if it differs, was never delivered and never will be; if it is the message delivered, the `REFUND` matched its record and paid it (§5.5b), which cleared the record. So every record left under `n` is of a message that never took effect.
2. *`VOID` from a veto.* `OrangeInbox` never delivers a vetoed nonce, and the entry stays `Vetoed`; nothing with `n` takes effect, now or later.
3. *An identity from a delivery.* `OrangeInbox`, or the OrangeSDK chain's inbox, delivered exactly `m` with nonce `n` (the stored hash, the entry's in `OrangeInbox` or the verifier's record on the OrangeSDK chain, commits to the whole message), and never delivers `n` again. If this gateway's record under `n` is `identity(m.payload)`, the message delivered is the one it sent, and `refundSpent` refuses (`NotSpent()`); otherwise its message `n` was not delivered and never will be. If the delivery of `m` is later undone, the `REFUND` that says so names exactly what `m` asked, matches the record and pays it under §5.5b, which clears it; the `VOID` it implies then finds no record.

In every case a record is refunded only when the message it records never took effect at the paired gateway and never will, and `refundSpent`, like a matching `REFUND`, clears the record, so a record pays at most once whichever path pays it. A nonce the paired gateway reports before this gateway has sent anything under it (a forgery of a nonce not yet used) stays spent: a later deposit that gets that nonce is refundable at once, which is right, as the inbox will never deliver it. A `REFUND` or `SPENT` that is sent again pays nothing by itself: the `REFUND` pays against the paired gateway's record, once, and a `SPENT` only records a fact. The guarantee rests on the inbox delivering each nonce once (spec 17 §6.1, spec 18 §6.5) and, for the reports, on `OrangeInbox` and the OrangeSDK chain's verifier keeping their records (spec 17 §6.3, spec 18 §6.4); a successor inbox starts above every nonce its predecessor could deliver (spec 17 §6.4), so the reports read only the inbox the gateway trusts now.

**What a forged channel back can do.** A forged `SPENT` or `REFUND` delivered to this gateway can make a record refundable while its message is delivered or still deliverable, and so pay its value twice: once on the other chain and once back to its sender here. That is no more than such a channel can do already: it can forge a `RELEASE` or `MINT` paying anyone, while a forged `SPENT` pays only recorded senders, each at most once, under the caps and limits of §6.2 and §6.5.

**Owners.** The owner of a gateway that received a forgery under the nonce of an honest message retires it with `refundQueued`, not `cancelQueued`: the refund tells the sending gateway at once that the nonce is spent. A forgery paid out at once, inside the limits, leaves nothing in the queue to refund; anyone reports it with `reportDelivered`, on either chain, from the hash its inbox recorded of what it delivered, and the message it displaced is refunded at its home. A forgery the owner cancelled without a refund is reported the same way: the inbox recorded its delivery, whatever the gateway undid.

**Why the record lives in the verifier.** On Ethereum `OrangeInbox` already keeps the hash of every message it proves. On the OrangeSDK chain the verifier used to mark a nonce delivered with one byte; it now writes the message's hash, which it computes anyway to check the proof, in the same entry, at one more 32-byte word of the native write charge (600 gas, spec 16 §6.1). Recording it in the gateway instead would cost every delivery a new storage slot (about 22 000 gas) and would miss forgeries to other recipients under a gateway's nonce. Neither record adds a power: a report only states what the inbox did, and the sending gateway decides against its own record, so the argument above holds unchanged for the new row: a record is refunded only when the message it records never took effect at the paired gateway and never will, and pays at most once.


### 5.6 Roles and their limits

| Role | Can | Cannot |
|---|---|---|
| anyone | deposit, deliver (through the channel), claim for a recipient, execute a queued transfer that is due and not frozen, `refundSpent` and `resendSpent` a message that never arrives (§5.5c), `reportDelivered` (§5.1, §5.2), on Ethereum `reportVetoed` | anything else |
| guardian | `pause()`, `freezeQueued(id)` (§5.5a) | unpause, unfreeze or cancel, change any parameter, move value |
| owner | `pause()`, `unpause()`, `freezeQueued(id)`, `unfreezeQueued(id)`, `cancelQueued(id)` (§5.5a), `refundQueued(id)` (§5.5b), `setLockCap(token, cap)`, `setMintCap(origin, cap)`, `setReleaseLimit(token, amount, window)`, `setMintLimit(origin, amount, window)` (§6.5), `hello()` (§5.7), `setGuardian` (never zero), `transferOwnership` (never zero); on Ethereum also `setInbox` and `setMessageGas` | mint, release, burn, take locked, owed or queued value, redirect or advance a queued transfer, refund to anyone but a recorded sender or more than once, change the paired gateway, the outbox or the creation code |

Others get `NotGuardian()` and `NotOwner()`. Pausing stops deposits, deliveries and claims; deliveries refused while paused wait in their channel. On the OrangeSDK chain the owner should be the chain's governance address (13 §9): the governance key's governed calls (`submitCall`, 13 §9.2) reach the owner functions at once under the key track, and under the stake track they are proposals that the validators and delegators vote on, the timelock, the guardian's veto and `reject` applying, so on a stake-governed chain no single key holds the caps, the limits or `unpause`; the guardian stays the quick way to pause. `crates/orange-bridge/tests/tokens_governance.rs` sets a release limit both ways on a gateway that names the governance address as its owner. On Ethereum the owner, like `OrangeInbox`'s, should be a timelock: `setInbox` is the one power that reaches value, since an inbox the owner chose could deliver anything, and the timelock gives holders time to see a change coming.

### 5.7 Deployment

Both gateways are deployed by ordinary transactions, the one on the OrangeSDK chain by any account, not placed by the kernel at genesis like the inbox of spec 18 §4. Each gateway needs the other's address in its constructor; both are known in advance from their deployers' nonces (`CREATE`), and either may be deployed first. The pair of addresses a chain publishes is its canonical bridge.

A message to a gateway that does not exist can lose what it carries. `OrangeInbox` delivers a message to an address without code as a success and consumes its nonce (spec 17 §6.1), so a `MINT` executed on Ethereum before the gateway exists there would do nothing and could never be delivered again, and what it locked on the OrangeSDK chain would stay locked for ever; the same would hold for good if the Ethereum deployer spent the predicted nonce on another transaction. In the other direction the inbox refuses a delivery to an address without code and the message stays deliverable (spec 18 §6.5), but it waits for the OrangeSDK-chain gateway, for ever if that is never deployed at its predicted address. **Each gateway therefore takes no deposit until a message of its paired gateway has arrived** (`peerLive`, §5.3, §5.4): a message the channel delivered from the paired address proves a gateway is there, speaking this protocol.

The *handshake* makes that message arrive without a deposit. Each constructor sends `HELLO(false)`, and a gateway that receives `HELLO(false)` answers `HELLO(true)`, which is answered by nothing:

- The OrangeSDK chain's gateway first: its `HELLO` is executed on Ethereum before a gateway is there, and is gone. Ethereum's gateway, deployed, sends its `HELLO`, which waits in the inbox until the OrangeSDK-chain gateway takes it; the OrangeSDK-chain gateway is confirmed and answers, and the answer confirms the Ethereum gateway.
- Ethereum's gateway first: its `HELLO` waits in the inbox. The OrangeSDK chain's gateway, deployed, sends its `HELLO`, which confirms the Ethereum gateway once executed there; its answer confirms the OrangeSDK-chain gateway. When the first `HELLO` is delivered later, the OrangeSDK-chain gateway answers it once more, harmlessly.

A gateway whose answer was lost (vetoed in `OrangeInbox`, say) stays unconfirmed; its owner calls `hello()`, which sends `HELLO(false)` again and reverts `AlreadyConfirmed()` once the gateway is confirmed. A gateway paired with an address where no gateway will ever be never hears from it and never locks anything. Owners still check the paired gateway's code and constructor arguments before listing tokens (§6.2): the handshake proves a gateway at the address, not that its caps, limits and roles are the ones they want.

Genesis placement would give every OrangeSDK chain the gateway at one reserved address, but it would put the gateway's code in the kernel (the inbox's runtime is embedded in it) and its configuration in genesis, make any change to it a kernel upgrade, and require a storage layout written without a constructor. Nothing about the gateway needs the kernel: it holds no privilege, calls the outbox and is called by the inbox as any contract is, and authenticates its peer by address. Unlike the inbox, which every OrangeSDK-chain contract must be able to trust at a fixed address, a gateway is trusted only by the holders of its own tokens.

## 6. Safety

### 6.1 The value invariant

For every token native to one side, with `locked` the gateway's `locked[token]` there and `minted` the `totalSupply` of its wrapped token on the other side:

```
locked = minted + Σ amount of MINT of the token not yet delivered + Σ amount of RELEASE of the token not yet delivered
balance of the gateway in the token = locked + Σ owed[token][·]
```

so `minted ≤ locked` always: a `MINT` is sent only after its tokens are locked, a `RELEASE` only after its wrapped tokens are burned, each message acts once, and a failed release keeps its tokens in the gateway. Queued transfers (§5.5a) extend both equalities: a queued mint counts beside `minted` (`locked = minted + mintsQueued + in flight`), and a queued release has left `locked` but not the gateway (`balance = locked + Σ owed + releasesQueued`). An honest queued transfer that the owner cancels without a refund stays locked at the token's home with nothing minted against it, like a vetoed message, so the first equality gains `+ cancelled` and the second is unchanged. One the owner refunds (§5.5b) never enters `cancelled`: its `REFUND` counts as in flight, a refund of a `MINT` as a `RELEASE` coming home and a refund of a `RELEASE` as a `MINT` leaving it, and once delivered it is the release or mint it acts as; so `cancelled` holds exactly the cancellations nobody refunded. A cancelled forgery leaves both as they were before it, refunded or not, since its `REFUND` is refused and counts for nothing. An honest message that never arrives (§5.5c), its nonce taken by a forgery or vetoed, counts as in flight for as long as its sender holds its record: what it carries is locked or burned at home with nothing paid out for it, exactly as while it travelled. `refundSpent` then pays it back as a delivered refund would, leaving both equalities as they were, and `resendSpent` puts its `REFUND` back in flight. A mint sent back for its zero cap (§5.4) is a refund in flight. The invariant assumes the channel delivers only messages the paired gateway sent, beyond what the owner cancels or refunds; `crates/orange-bridge/tests/tokens_contracts.rs` checks both equalities, with the queues, the refunds in flight, the messages that never arrive and what was cancelled, after every step of a randomized run of 900 deposits, burns, deliveries in random order (some failing on a pause or a cap and retried), pauses, cap changes (some to zero, which sends mints back), refusing recipients, passing time, executions of queued transfers under rate limits on every token, the guardian freezing, the owner unfreezing, cancelling and refunding single queued transfers while the others run on (some executions refused as frozen, some refunds refused as transfers a refund queued), forged `MINT`s and `RELEASE`s that the channels deliver, the guardian freezes and the owner cancels or refunds, their refunds refused by the home gateway; forgeries that take the nonce of an honest `MINT`, `RELEASE`, `REFUND` or `SPENT` in flight, queued (or sent back for a zero cap) or, into either chain, a `HELLO` paid out at once; vetoes of messages from the OrangeSDK chain; and reports of all of them on either chain, and refunds and resends of what never arrives, refused until the sender knows the nonce spent. At the end every stranded message is made good but those whose sender was never told, which a forgery the owner cancelled without a refund leaves. `one_queued_transfer_is_frozen_or_cancelled_alone` delivers a forged `MINT` and a forged `RELEASE` and checks that freezing and cancelling each restores both equalities exactly while honest transfers keep flowing, refunds an honest cancelled mint and release to their senders exactly once, has the home gateway refuse the refunds of a forged mint, a forged release and a forged mint that took an honest message's nonce, and then refunds the honest message whose nonce was taken; `messages_that_never_arrive_are_refunded_or_sent_again` refunds a deposit whose nonce a queued forgery took, a deposit from either chain whose nonce a forgery paid out at once took, and a vetoed one, sends again a `REFUND` whose nonce a forgery took, sends an unlisted mint back, and refuses every report, refund and resend that the rules of §5.1 and §5.5c refuse; and `tests/tokens.rs` checks them end to end, a refund, a forgery under a dishonest sync committee, a veto and a mint sent back included.

### 6.2 Caps

`lockCap[token]` bounds what the gateway may hold locked of a native token, and a token whose cap is zero cannot be deposited at all: a token is *listed* on a gateway when its lock cap is non-zero, and the owner lists only tokens that behave (§6.3). `mintCap[origin]` bounds the supply of a wrapped token on the receiving side. A cap lowered below what is outstanding refuses new deposits or mints, never touches what exists, and a mint over its cap waits in its channel until the owner raises the cap. A mint cap of zero means the token is not listed here: its mints are sent back to be refunded (§5.4) rather than left in the channel. So a mint over a non-zero cap is retried, which suffices while the owner means to accept the token, and an owner who never will sets its cap to zero, which sends back every mint delivered from then on, those already waiting included. A refund that mints (of a `RELEASE`, §5.5b) is never sent back: it waits for its cap as a mint does, since there is nowhere further back to send it.

The caps bound a forgery in amount. If a channel delivers a message the paired gateway never sent (a dishonest sync-committee supermajority, or a threshold of shareholders certifying a false root), the receiving gateway mints at most up to the cap of each wrapped token, or releases what is locked, which the lock cap bounds. Every gateway message carries value 0, so `OrangeInbox`'s `maxValuePerWindow` (spec 17 §6.2) never counts bridged tokens, and the channels' rate limits (spec 17 §6.2, spec 18 §6.4) count messages, not amounts; the owners of the channels give each gateway a message limit of its own there, so that messages anyone sends to other recipients cannot fill the window the gateway's messages need, and since a gateway refuses every message but its peer's, nobody else can fill its own; the gateways' own rate limits (§6.5) bound a forgery in time: what a forged message asks beyond a limit is queued, the guardian freezes it (or pauses the gateway) before it is paid out, and the owner cancels it, which undoes its delivery (§5.5a). What the channels add against a forgery is time too: the delay of `OrangeInbox` (spec 17 §6.2), during which its guardian can pause it and veto the message (spec 17 §6.3), and the delay of the OrangeSDK chain's light client (spec 18 §6.3), during which governance can pause deliveries and revoke the root (spec 18 §2); and the limit on how many messages a window delivers. Owners size caps to the value they accept at the channel's trust, and limits to the value they accept to lose before someone pauses.

### 6.3 Tokens

- **Fee on transfer.** A deposit is refused (`BalanceMismatch()`) unless the gateway's balance grew by exactly the amount: a token that takes a fee, or credits less than it says, would otherwise mint more than it locks. Fee-taking tokens are not supported.
- **Rebasing and other balance changes.** A token whose balances change without transfers is not supported, and must not be listed. A positive rebase leaves a surplus in the gateway that nobody can take; a negative one makes `balance < locked + owed`, and the last releases fail and are owed until the gateway holds enough. Nothing in the gateway detects a rebase.
- **Tokens that lie.** A token whose `balanceOf` lies, or that moves balances arbitrarily, defeats any check; listing is the defense.
- **Reentrancy.** Every entry point that moves value (`deposit`, receiving, `claim`, `claimTo`, `executeQueued`, `refundSpent`) holds one transient-storage lock and reverts `Reentrant()` when entered again, so a token's hook or a recipient cannot re-enter the gateway; state is updated before each external transfer.
- **Recipients that revert or burn gas.** A release calls the recipient or the token with `TRANSFER_GAS` and copies no unbounded return data, so a hostile recipient can neither take the gateway's remaining gas nor make the delivery revert; its release is owed (§5.5).

### 6.4 What the gateways add, and what they do not change

The gateways add per-token caps and rate limits, their own pause, refusal of unlisted and fee-taking tokens, the handshake, queued transfers and owed releases. They change nothing in the channels: the light clients, the inboxes' delays, rate limits, pauses and vetoes, and the roles that govern them apply to token messages as to any other. A channel's limit on value per window does not reach tokens, whose messages carry value 0; its limit on messages per window does (§6.2). An `OrangeInbox` veto of a token message drops it for ever: a vetoed `RELEASE` leaves its wrapped tokens burned and the origin locked, a vetoed `MINT` leaves its tokens locked. Both keep the invariant (`minted ≤ locked`), and anyone reports the veto to the gateway on the OrangeSDK chain (`reportVetoed`, §5.1), which then refunds the message to its sender (§5.5c). The OrangeSDK chain's inbox has no veto: governance revokes a light-client root (spec 18 §2), and the messages it had not delivered are proven again under the next roots, so nothing honest is lost to a revocation; a nonce a forged root delivered stays delivered, which is a forgery taking a nonce (§5.5c).

### 6.5 Rate limits

Each gateway limits what it pays out, per token: releases of each native token (`releaseLimit[token]`) and mints of each wrapped token (`mintLimit[origin]`), keyed as the lock and mint caps are. Both gateways run the same `TokenGateway`, so the limits hold on both sides: on the OrangeSDK chain the gateway limits the wrapped tokens it mints and the currency and tokens it releases, and on Ethereum the same; a forged message on either channel meets the limit of the gateway it reaches.

A limit is `RateLimit { amount, window, updated, used }`: at most `amount` paid out at once, refilling at `amount` per `window` seconds, a bucket. `used` is what has been paid out or scheduled, less what has refilled up to `updated`. When a mint or release of `x` arrives at time `t` (`block.timestamp`, seconds):

```
used' = max(0, used − ⌊(t − updated) · amount / window⌋) + x
readyAt = t                                           if used' ≤ amount
        = t + ⌈(used' − amount) · window / amount⌉    otherwise
```

and the limit stores `used'` and `updated = t`. A transfer due at `t` is paid out at once; any other is queued until `readyAt` (§5.5a). Over any interval of `T` seconds the transfers that become due amount to at most `amount · (1 + T / window)`, however they arrive, and a transfer larger than `amount` waits until enough has refilled instead of being refused. A zero `amount` sets no limit and leaves `used` alone; arithmetic that overflows reverts the delivery, which then stays deliverable (§5.4), and so does a `readyAt` above `2^64 − 1`, with `QueueTooLong()`: kept in 64 bits it would wrap to a time at or before `t`, and a transfer that should wait for ever would be paid out at once. A limit whose `window / amount` is so large that a transfer within the caps waits past `2^64` seconds keeps that transfer in its channel until the owner sets a limit it fits.

The owner sets a limit with `setReleaseLimit(token, amount, window)` or `setMintLimit(origin, amount, window)`, logging `ReleaseLimitChanged` or `MintLimitChanged`; a non-zero `amount` with a zero `window` reverts `BadWindow()`. A change counts what the old limit had used, refilled up to now (none when there was no limit), against the new one; transfers already queued keep their `readyAt`. Limits start at zero, no limit: owners set one when they list a token (§6.2), sized to what they accept to be paid out before the guardian pauses, and change it as governance decides (§5.6).

**Windows in time, not blocks.** The two chains make blocks at different rates (Ethereum every 12 seconds, an OrangeSDK chain every `slot_ms`, which its governance may change), and what a limit is for, the time a guardian needs to see a forgery and pause, is wall-clock time, as the channels' own delays are. A limit by block height would mean a different time on each side and change with the slot. A block's timestamp is its proposer's, within what the chain's validators accept (on Ethereum, its slot's time), and a skew of `s` seconds moves what refills by `s · amount / window`, small against a window of hours.

**Queue, not refuse.** The receiving gateway cannot refuse before the value is locked: the lock happened on the other chain when the message was sent. Reverting the delivery, as a cap does, would leave the message in its channel to be retried, by relayers who cannot know when a retry will pass, for as long as traffic keeps the limit full; and a channel that delivers each message once (`OrangeInbox` after its veto window) has no other place to keep it. Queuing consumes the message, fixes when the value is paid out from the order of arrivals, and lets anyone pay it out then; nothing waits on a relayer, and nothing is lost.

## 7. Relaying

Token messages are relayed exactly as any message of their channel (spec 17 §7, spec 18 §6); a relayer needs nothing token-specific. `orange_bridge::tokens` adds:

- `wrapped_token` (§4);
- calldata for `deposit`, `claim`, `claimTo`, `setLockCap`, `setMintCap`, `setReleaseLimit`, `setMintLimit`, `executeQueued`, `freezeQueued`, `unfreezeQueued`, `cancelQueued`, `refundQueued`, `refundSpent`, `resendSpent`, `reportVetoed`, `reportDelivered` on either gateway (`report_delivered_calldata`, `report_delivered_on_orange_calldata`) and single-address views (`locked`, `lockCap`, `wrappedToken`, `balanceOf`, `mintsQueued`, `releasesQueued`);

## 10. Limits of this version

- **Unsupported tokens** (§6.3): fee-on-transfer, rebasing and otherwise self-changing balances. ERC-777 hooks and ERC-1363 callbacks are harmless (the lock refuses reentrance) but gain nothing.
- **No relayer fee.** Deliveries are paid by whoever relays them; a user can relay their own.
- **No permit, no deposit-and-call.** A deposit needs a prior `approve`; a message carries no call for the recipient to run.
- **Metadata is fixed** at creation (§4); a token that changes its name keeps the old one on the other side.
- **The owner of the Ethereum gateway can change its inbox** (§5.6), which is needed to follow an `OrangeInbox` succession but is a power over value; a timelock is the protection.
- **Limits are off until set** (§6.5): a token listed without a limit is bounded only by its cap, as before; nothing forces an owner to set one.
- **`cancelQueued` is final** (§5.5a): an honest transfer cancelled without a refund stays locked at its home. A forgery cancelled without a refund does not by itself tell the sending gateway that the nonce it took is spent; anyone still reports the delivery with `reportDelivered` (§5.1, §5.2). Owners retire suspect transfers with `refundQueued`, freezing first while they decide.
- **Deliveries recorded before 2026-11-06 cannot be reported on the OrangeSDK chain** (§5.2): a nonce that an earlier verifier marked delivered with one byte reads as the word 1 in `deliveredHash`, which no message hashes to. No network was launched before then (`ops/networks.json`).
- **A refund that mints waits for its cap** (§6.2): the refund of a `RELEASE`, by `REFUND` or `refundSpent`, mints under the mint cap and is never sent back.
- **Reports read the current inbox** (§5.1): after `setInbox` moves the Ethereum gateway to a successor, vetoes and deliveries of the old inbox can no longer be reported, and messages the old inbox never delivered below the successor's `firstNonce` (spec 17 §6.4) never arrive and are not refunded.
- **Records are kept for ever**: every deposit, `REFUND` and `SPENT` leaves one storage slot in its gateway (§5.3), cleared only when the message is refunded or sent again, and `spent` and `reported` keep a slot per reported nonce. Clearing a record once the receiving side confirms delivery would cost a confirmation message for every message, with its proof and relaying on the other chain and its storage there, against one slot that the channel's own outbox already matches on Ethereum (`EthereumOutbox` keeps every message's hash for ever, spec 18 §6.1); so the growth is accepted: one storage slot, about 22 000 gas, per message, paid by the transaction that sends it.
- **The handshake proves a gateway, not its configuration** (§5.7): a peer deployed with other caps, limits or roles is confirmed all the same.
- `TokenMessage` decodes amounts up to `2^128 − 1`; a larger amount is valid on chain but does not decode in the helper.

## 11. Rationale (non-normative)

- **One gateway contract for both sides.** Locking, minting, burning, releasing, caps, owed releases and the invariant are the same on both chains; only sending and authenticating differ. One audited body of code, two thin adapters.
- **Lock and mint, not mint against the outbox's value.** The outbox keeps value for ever, and the OrangeSDK chain's currency must be releasable on the way back; the gateway is the custodian on each side.
- **Refuse instead of measuring.** A gateway could credit the balance delta of a fee-taking token instead of refusing it, but the fee would be taken again on release, and the next holders would bear it; refusing keeps one token in for one minted.
- **Owed instead of reverting.** A release that reverted the whole delivery would leave a message stuck for as long as its recipient refuses, perhaps for ever, with the channel's nonce never consumed. Recording the debt consumes the message and keeps the value claimable.
- **Metadata in every mint.** Channels do not order messages; any mint may be the first to arrive.
- **A bucket, not fixed windows.** A limit counted per fixed window lets twice the limit through across a window's end; the refilling bucket of §6.5 bounds every interval, and its state is two words per token.
- **The sender decides a refund, from its own record.** The receiving gateway cannot tell an honest message from a forged one, which is what the channel is for; the sending gateway can, since it knows what it sent. So the owner on the receiving side may ask for a refund of any cancelled transfer, and the sender pays only against its record (§5.5b). The outboxes already keep a hash of every message (spec 17 §3, spec 18 §6.1), but of the whole message, with the metadata and gas the receiving gateway does not keep, and the OrangeSDK-chain outbox's storage is not readable by a contract; one slot per deposit, about 22 500 gas, buys refunds that no forgery can turn into value.
- **Confirmed by any message, answered once.** Any authenticated message proves the peer, so a gateway that hears a mint first needs no `HELLO`; answering only a `HELLO(false)` ends the exchange after one answer, whatever the order of deployment.
- **The receiving side reports facts, the sender decides.** What became of a nonce is known only where it was delivered, and what was sent under it only where it was sent. So the receiving side reports the fact, from its queue (a `REFUND` that does not match) or from its inbox's record (a veto or a delivery), and the sender compares it with its own record before paying. Neither side alone can be tricked into paying for a message that arrives: the receiving gateway never pays, and the sending gateway pays only for a nonce its paired gateway says was spent by something other than what it recorded.
- **Refund, not resend, a message that never arrives.** Sending a `MINT` or `RELEASE` again under a new nonce would complete the transfer, but over the channel a forgery just went through, to a recipient who may no longer want it, and with metadata read again; refunding returns the value to the one account that certainly wants it, through the gateway's own limits. A `REFUND` or `SPENT` has nothing to return and is sent again instead, which is harmless as both are idempotent.
- **Send back an unlisted mint, retry one over its cap.** A zero cap is the owner's statement that the token is not accepted; leaving its mints in the channel would hold the sender's value until the owner changed their mind. A non-zero cap is a bound the owner raises as traffic grows, and sending back every mint that meets it momentarily would turn a wait into a round trip.
